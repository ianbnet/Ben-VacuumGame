# What changed on 7 November 2026

Everything in `docs/assessment-2026-11-07.md` was applied, in three sprints, on the branch `overhaul/tiers-1-3`. The state before any of this is tagged `pre-overhaul-2026-11-07` on `master`.

To go back: `git checkout master` (or `git checkout pre-overhaul-2026-11-07`).

## Tier 1: bugs and quick wins

- One `tryMove()` movement rule shared by both views; first-person reverse no longer duplicates it.
- Camera heading is reset with the level and re-synced when switching views (bug A).
- Turning in 3D never auto-repeats; forward and back repeat at 180 ms instead of 120 ms (bug B).
- Level Complete and Out of Battery render over the 3D scene instead of cutting to 2D (bug C).
- Restart keeps the current view from both the R key and the button (bug D).
- Held keys are released on window blur (bug F).
- Score is snapshotted at level start and restored on Restart.
- Canvas backing store scaled by `devicePixelRatio`; floor patterns drawn at device resolution.
- All eases, fades, the dust motes and the ghost step timer run on elapsed time.
- Level-name banner with the goal line (first level) and your best result; level name in the panel.
- Help overlay (H or ?), key legend under the panel, buttons blur after click.
- Level Complete shows stars, moves vs par and the score breakdown; the bonus is 200 + 10 per move under par.
- The unreadable "CLEAN" label on the Roomba is gone.

## Tier 2: the 3D view, layout, progress

- New renderer (`render3d.js`): one ray per pixel column into an `ImageData` buffer, about 2 ms a frame.
- Rays continue past low furniture, so walls and the ceiling show above a couch; table tops are open underneath.
- Textured walls (brick, matching the 2D wall tile), couch, table, chair, plant, pet bed, ceiling and dock; the floor texture is the 2D tile artwork.
- Warm haze instead of blue fog; the Roomba's eye is low (0.28 of a wall) and pitched slightly down.
- In-view HUD: compass strip, tiles left, dock distance with a head-back warning, battery bar with low-battery pulse and vignette, larger minimap, the Roomba's own bumper, camera shake on bump.
- The tile a forward press reaches is highlighted; a short fade on view switch.
- D-pad relabels to turn/drive in 3D; dock distance in the panel; `aria` labels and a live status line.
- Layout scales to phone width with a 56–68 px d-pad, `touch-action: none`, and the page scrolls on short windows.
- Progress saved to `localStorage`; level select row with stars; resume at the furthest level.

## Tier 3: extras and structure

- Plants and chairs are billboard sprites with transparency.
- Synthesised sound (`audio.js`): motor hum, tile tick, bump, dock chime, low-battery beep, level fanfare, game over; M mutes and the setting is remembered.
- The ghost replay can be watched in 3D (V during the replay).
- `game.js` split into `core.js`, `render2d.js`, `render3d.js`, `audio.js` and `game.js`.
- `tests/run.js` checks every level's data, the par solver's route, and the movement, scoring and progression rules headlessly.
- README.
- The frame loop survives a thrown error (reported once to the console) instead of freezing the game.

## Deliberately not changed

- The title still says "Roomba Vacuum". Renaming is a decision for whoever publishes it.
- The par route is still the greedy nearest-tile solver. It is beatable, which the Level Complete screen now celebrates.

- The static parts of the board are painted once per level into offscreen layers (`scene2d.js`): a hardwood floor of varied planks with grain, knots and seams, ambient occlusion along the walls, baseboards; the furniture with soft cast shadows from the window light (walls get a lip so they stand up); and a warm window light pool with blind stripes drawn with an overlay blend.

## Later the same day: ghost any time, upgrades

- The ghost replay is on G (and a Ghost button) and works before, during or after a level; it leaves the player's game untouched and returns to it when it ends. W is movement only.
- Upgrade points: every new star earned on a level is one point (so up to 18 across the six rooms). The Upgrades panel (U) spends them on five upgrades across two groups. Vacuum: efficient motor (15/30/45% less drain), bigger battery (125/150), solar cells (trickle-charge on the move). Charger: charging pad (the tiles around the dock charge too; the dock-distance hint measures to the nearest charging tile) and overcharge (leave the dock at 115/130%). Refund all gives every point back.
- Battery is now shown as charge / capacity, low battery is 20% of capacity, and the charging pad is drawn on the board and in 3D. The Level Complete card shows the points earned.

## Later the same day: 3D glow-up

- `render3d.js` rewritten around light and reflection. The buffer renders at 1.5x the canvas size (dropping a notch automatically if frames average over 14 ms) with 256 px materials: warm plaster room walls with a baseboard and picture rail, brick for interior walls, a real window on the north wall with sky and garden where the 2D light comes from, a ceiling with a lamp, and a floor baked from the same planks as the 2D board so the boards run across tiles, with dust on dirty tiles and polish on clean ones.
- A lightmap is baked per level at 8 cells a tile: ambient, the window as an area light with soft shadows (walls block it, low furniture most of it), the ceiling lamp, the dock's blue glow, and ambient occlusion where the floor meets walls. It is blurred and sampled bilinearly on walls and sprites and with an ordered dither on the floor and ceiling. The vacuum's own light colour glows on the floor around it; the ghost glows cyan.
- Polished tiles reflect: every wall, piece of furniture and sprite is drawn again flipped into the floor beneath it, faded with depth and distance. Dirty wood does not reflect, so cleaning reveals the room in the floor.
- Dust motes drift in the window light as tiny emissive sprites; a soft shaft of light appears when the window is in view; a bloom pass makes the window, lamp and lights glow; a grade cools the ceiling, warms the floor and darkens the edges; the bumper shows a hint of the room reflected in its hood.
- Head-bob while driving. Switching views is a dive: the board zooms into the Roomba and dissolves into the first-person frame, and lifts back out the other way.
- Frame cost on this Mac: 11 to 15 ms at 1.5x. `fpBakeLightmap` is pure and covered by tests.

## Later the same day: table legs, full screen

- Tables in 3D stand on four turned wooden legs, one in from each corner of the footprint. They are billboard sprites from the floor to the underside of the top, lit by the lightmap and reflected in polished tiles like everything else. The underside of a table is now painted back to front in depth segments so the legs under it sit at their true depth instead of being covered by the slab.
- Full screen: Enter, Escape or the Full screen button. The browser's full-screen API is used where it exists (with a fixed full-viewport layout as the fallback on browsers without it, such as Safari on iPhone). The title and legend make way, the controls stack in a 320 px side column that scrolls if it has to, and the board grows to fill the remaining height or width, whichever runs out first; portrait screens stack the board above the controls instead. The canvas backing store follows the displayed density (up to 3x) so the board stays sharp when it is drawn larger than 720 px, and the 2D scene layers rebuild themselves for the new density. The 3D renderer's internal resolution is unchanged, so its frame cost is unchanged.

## 2026-11-08: the GPU renderer, in a 1950s atomic-age style

The first-person view moved from the software raycaster to a Three.js renderer over WebGL2
(`render3d-gl.js`, with Three.js r170 vendored under `vendor/three/` and loaded through an import
map: still no build step, still static hosting). The raycaster stays as "Classic" in the design
panel and takes over on its own if the GPU renderer cannot start or keeps failing. Gameplay is
untouched. The six sprints of `docs/assessment-3d-aaa-2026-11-08.md`:

- **A. Foundation.** Scene built per level from `scene3d.js`, a plain-data description of the room
  that the tests check: themes, furniture from parts, architecture, decor. PBR materials from
  procedural canvases; a shadow-mapped sun through the window and blinds; hemisphere, bounce and
  image-based fill (the room is captured once per level into an environment map); ACES tone
  mapping, SMAA, bloom. The camera comes from the existing view model, the HUD is drawn on top,
  and the floor's wood is the same plank painter as the 2D board.
- **B. Set dressing, atomic age.** Six themed rooms: Atomic Lounge (morning), Rocket Nursery
  (golden hour), Chrome Diner (noon, checkerboard lino), Mission Control (afternoon, walnut
  panelling), Hangar 51 (dusk, concrete, crates, a rocket on its gantry outside) and Night Lab
  (night, neon panels, a moon). A couch on tapered legs with brass ferrules and tufted cushions,
  a kidney coffee table on three splayed legs, a moulded shell chair on a wire base, a snake plant
  in a bullet planter, a pet bed with a sleeping cat that breathes, kitchen counters, crates and
  neon panels for the interior walls, a porthole door, venetian blinds, a Sputnik chandelier, a
  starburst clock that keeps real time, framed boomerang, starburst, rocket and atom prints, a
  chrome saucer dock with a blinking beacon, a flying saucer outside, crumbs and fluff on dirty
  tiles that vanish as they are cleaned, and the vacuum's own bumper, ring light and lens in frame.
- **C. Light.** The sun carries each room; clouds drift across it. Clean tiles turn glossy and
  reflect the room through a planar mirror render blended by a per-tile mask (dirty tiles stay
  matte under their dust); ground-truth ambient occlusion; the flashlight is a spotlight with
  shadows; the dock, the chandelier and the vacuum's ring light are real lights; a translucent
  prism of sun hangs in the air between the window and the floor, with dust motes lit only where
  they cross it.
- **D. Lens.** Depth of field that focuses on what is ahead, exposure that settles when you turn
  into or away from the window, a bank into turns with a touch of motion blur, head-bob, grain,
  vignette and a hint of chromatic aberration. Drag on the board to look around while stopped; the
  view eases back when you drive.
- **E. Life.** Dust is drawn into the intake as a tile comes clean and a glint sweeps the tile.
  The dive between views is a real camera flight: from an overhead shot that matches the board
  down into the vacuum's eye, and back up. The HUD is hairline type with no boxes and fades after
  three seconds without input; the compass stays faintly.
- **F. Hardening.** Quality presets (Low, Medium, High, Ultra) in the design panel switch
  resolution scale, shadow size, AO, depth of field and reflections; the internal resolution also
  adapts to frame time. Tests cover the scene description (footprints, legs, headroom, window,
  door, chandelier, dock, sun direction, overhead camera).

Not verified: Safari and Firefox, mobile GPUs, true GPU frame time (the preview pane runs the loop
at a reduced rate, so only CPU submit time, 5 to 10 ms, was measurable).

## Later on 2026-11-08: the board in the same atomic-age style

The 2D view now shows the same six rooms as the GPU renderer, from above. Nothing about play
changed: tiles, dust and polish, the dock, the charging pad, the ghost and its trail, the
flashlight, the overlays, the banner and the help screen all do what they did.

- **Themes.** `scene2d.js` reads each level's theme from `scene3d.js` (time of day, sun, sky, walls
  and accent wall, trim, floor, lamp, door, furniture colours) and derives what a top-down view
  needs (`s2Sun`, `s2Shadow`, `s2Palette`, pure and tested): where the sun's light lands on the
  floor (each height on the window divided by the sun's slope), which parts are striped by the
  half-drawn blinds, where the mullions' shadows cut it, how long and how dark shadows are, and
  which way they fall. When the sun is too low to matter and the lamp is on, shadows fall away
  from the lamp instead.
- **Floors.** Plank wood (the same painter as the 3D floor, so the boards match), checkerboard
  lino for the Diner, sealed concrete with expansion joints and a safety stripe for the Hangar;
  a tint for golden hour, dusk and night. A faint tile grid keeps moves countable on every floor.
- **Room shell.** The wall caps around the room in the wall and accent colours with skirting, the
  window with its sky, mullions and sill, the porthole door in its own colour with the arc it
  swings through drawn on the floor.
- **Furniture, painted once per level.** Sofa on a walnut frame with arms, a tufted bolster, seat
  cushions and a throw pillow; kidney coffee table (the 3D outline) with a gloss sweep, three
  splayed legs on brass ferrules, a dish and a magazine; moulded shell chair on a wire base;
  snake plant in a bullet planter as a rosette of blades; oval pet bed with a bolster and a cat
  that breathes; and the interior walls per theme: plaster partitions, kitchen counters with a hob
  and a sink, stacked crates with a stencilled starburst, neon panels glowing pink and cyan.
  Everything turns to face the nearest wall, as in 3D.
- **Light.** A cached layer holds the sun patch (blurred, striped, cut by the mullions and the
  transom), the sky's glow at the window, a soft wash across the room and the lamp's pool; a slow
  cloud passes over the sun. The Sputnik itself hangs between us and the floor, drawn lightly.
  Polished tiles mirror the lamp and the sky, clipped to the clean tiles, so cleaning reveals the
  ceiling in the floor. Dust takes the floor's tone (dark grime on concrete).
- **Dock and vacuum.** A chrome saucer with a pulsing blue ring, a fin with a blinking beacon and a
  rubber ramp, the bolt etched in brass. The vacuum is the 3D one from above: dark lacquered hood,

- **Tests.** `tests/run.js` now loads the 2D files too and checks, for every room, that the sun comes

## 2026-11-09: three ways to keep playing

From the ten ideas in `docs/assessment-fun-2026-11-09.md`, the three the judge picked, plus the
judge's own idea: their options live on a bar at the top of the page.

- **Race my best.** Every run is recorded as its moves. Finishing a room saves the run when it
  beats the one on file. The next time, a gold ghost drives the best run, one step for each of the
  player's, and a "Vs your best" readout counts tiles ahead or behind at the same move. A ghost
  whose run is over fades. It shows in 2D, as a see-through gold vacuum in the GPU 3D view, and as
  a dot on both 3D minimaps. B or the switch on the bar turns it off. The par ghost on G is
  unchanged.
- **Room of the day.** `generateDailyRoom(date)` rolls a room from the calendar date: a style
  (open, divided, maze-like), a battery cost, a dock, then walls, sofas, tables, chairs, plants and
  a pet bed, each checked as it goes down so nothing overlaps, the dock keeps clear tiles around
  it and no floor is ever walled off. A layout is kept only if it validates, has at least 96 tiles,
  and its par is under 2.7 moves a tile; otherwise the next roll is tried. The theme rotates by
  day. The same date gives the same room on any device. The bar steps back through a year of
  earlier days. There is no streak, no reward for consecutive days and no penalty for skipping.
- **Build a room.** An editor on the board with a palette of eight tools, a Turn switch for sofas
  and pet beds, a theme and a battery difficulty. Clicks place, drags paint walls or erase and fill
  in every tile the pointer crossed. `validateRoom` runs after every change: walled-off tiles are
  hatched in coral, tiles too far for a round trip on one charge in amber, and the room cannot be
  played or shared until both are gone. Rooms save as they are built, up to 24. A room is stored
  and shared as a short URL-safe code (`encodeRoom` / `decodeRoom`), so **Copy link** needs no
  server; opening a link loads the room unsaved, with **Keep this room** to file it. Editing a
  room's layout retires its best run, because a run belongs to a layout.
- **The bar at the top.** The House, Room of the Day, Build a Room, and the Race my best switch.
  The level buttons moved into it. In full screen it moves into the side column so the board keeps
  its height.
- **Under the hood.** Extra rooms load into one slot after the six house levels, so the 2D view
  and both 3D renderers draw them from the same data; what each renderer caches per level is
  thrown away when the slot changes. A room carries its own theme. Long pieces now keep their
  backs along their long side (`s3Facing`), so a sofa is never drawn end-on to a wall. The par
  solver takes a room object as well as a level index and stops instead of looping when a room
  cannot be finished. Rooms beyond the house keep a best run and pay no upgrade points, so the
  house stays the source of upgrades.
- **Tests.** 56 new checks: room codes and their refusals, validation, the placement rules, dates,
  ninety consecutive generated days, a pinned date, recorded runs and the ghost, and every room
  mode including shared links.

Not verified: Safari and Firefox, a real touch screen in the editor (pointer events were driven by
synthetic clicks and drags), and the clipboard path of Copy link (the preview pane refuses the
clipboard, so only the fallback that shows the link was seen).

## 2026-11-09: published

The game is live at <https://robot-vacuum-game.netlify.app>, in its own Netlify project.

- `scripts/publish-netlify.mjs` uploads an explicit set of files through Netlify's API: `index.html`,
  whatever it loads, and the vendored three.js. It starts from git's tracked files, so an ignored or
  stray file cannot ship, and it checks that every import a shipped file makes lands on another
  shipped file.
- After uploading it fetches every file from the live address and compares the bytes and the
  JavaScript content type with the local copies.
- `npm run deploy` runs the tests first. `npm run deploy:check` lists what would ship without
  touching the network.
- The Netlify team puts new sites behind team login by default. This site has that switched off so
  anyone with the address can play; the team default itself is unchanged.
- Checked on the live host: the house in 2D with real key presses, a room share link, and the GPU
  first-person view.

## 2026-11-09: thirty rooms

The house grew from six rooms to thirty: five floors of six. Rooms 1 to 6 are unchanged.

- `levels.js` holds rooms 7 to 30 as text maps (one letter per tile) and the reader that turns a
  map into the same level form the first six use. A malformed map throws, and the tests read
  every one.
- A room takes its look from its place on the floor, so each floor walks through the six themes
  and ends in the Night Lab.
- Difficulty is measured, not guessed. "Trips home at par" is how often the par route must come
  back to charge; "spare reach" is how many tiles of slack the farthest tile leaves on a full
  battery. Each floor asks more than the one below, each floor's last room is its hardest, and
  room 30 is the hardest in the house. It is the only room that drains four per move, which gives
  25 moves a charge, and its farthest tiles sit exactly at the limit.
- Every room is checked three ways: no tile is walled off, every tile can be reached and returned
  from on one standard charge, and the par route is driven through the real movement rules with
  no upgrades until the room is clean.
- The level picker shows one floor at a time and follows the room being played. Its arrows browse
  the other floors without leaving the room.
- A save from the six-room house that had finished room 6 finds room 7 open.
